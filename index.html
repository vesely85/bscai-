Why paper stopped winning

The old model

  • Retrospective — proof assembled after the fact
  • Sampled — a few inspections stood in for everything
  • Disputable — your word against the complaint
  • Slow — problems surfaced weeks later

What buyers expect now

  • Watch the work as it happens
  • Time-stamped, photo-evidenced audit trail
  • Live dashboards — in operations and in the bid room
  • Issues surfaced the same shift, not next month
The big shift: a building is now a stream of live data — foot traffic, lighting, HVAC, restroom dispensers — and every signal can call for service where it's needed, or tell you a space is fine to skip.

1

Configurability

Validation that matches the contract.
The customer demand
Every contract scopes work differently. Validation that can't mirror the contract creates noise, not proof.
What good looks like
  • Task depth and detail set per site and per scope
  • Routing rules — who validates what, in what order
  • Evidence requirements per task: photo, time, notes
  • Changes ship in days, not release cycles
Every location, one workflow — restrooms, break rooms, docks and more, each QR-verified and validated in the app.

2

UI ease

Crews actually use it.
The customer demand
Adoption is the real test. If the frontline won't use it, the data doesn't exist.
What good looks like
  • Mobile-first — works like the apps crews already know
  • Natural-language task lists, multilingual
  • Works offline and in poor-connectivity buildings
  • Minimal training to first completed route
From the field

Crews with minimal formal training hit 100% route completion on Day 1 of go-live — because validation lived inside the app they already clocked in with.Adoption by default, not by mandate.

3

Customer access

Customers see the work — in real time.
The customer demand
Service validation is customer-facing. Your client's leadership will look at this screen.
What good looks like
  • Client-facing dashboards, separate from internal ops views
  • Security permissions per role and per site
  • Public QR — occupants report issues the same shift
  • Filter by route, shift, supervisor or location
From the field

A plant manager reviewed live validation dashboards and requested new views on the spot — customer feedback that shaped the product roadmap.Transparency became the sales pitch.

4

System integration

Flows into the stack you already run.
The customer demand
Validation data in a silo is one more seam to reconcile. It should land where you run the business.
What good looks like
  • Tied to time & labor — same clock-in, same identity
  • Job costing and GL — evidence attributed to the job
  • HR and training records connected
  • Client ticketing systems in the loop
From the field

One app for the employee — clock in, see the route, validate the work. One data spine for the operator — every event tied to time, job cost and HR.The whole workforce operating system, one front door.

Proof: one app, whole workforce

The operating impact — measured across the workforce

When paper checklists become in-app validation, hours come back to the workforce. Cleaners stop re-handling and re-filing forms; supervisors stop rebuilding the day from clipboards, spreadsheets and phone calls to answer “was this done?”

And it holds up where the work happens — every scan, photo and timestamp is captured on the device even with no Wi-Fi or cellular, then syncs when signal returns. Nothing is lost, nothing is re-entered.

Proof: cleaning that answers to flights

A major Canadian airport — 10-year contract

The airport invited innovation on a decade-long industrial cleaning bid. The winning answer replaced static schedules with a demand engine: live flight data generates and assigns work as gates cycle through arrivals and departures.

Every gate cleaning captures pre/post photos and time-on-task. Dispatchers reassign work in real time as flight schedules shift.
